***Measure actual business health. Learn from the customers who matter most. Adjust based on proven impact.***

## The problem

Companies are handing more customer decisions to AI agents: answering questions, sending offers, granting discounts and free shipping. Three questions get harder to answer as they do:

- **Is the business actually healthier?** Page views, sessions and adoption can all rise while the best customers quietly leave.
- **What are the agents contributing?** Agents choose actions *because* of a customer's situation, so simple comparisons credit or blame them for the wrong things.
- **Can we prove what happened?** An instruction inside an agent's prompt leaves no trace in conventional systems, so differential treatment of customers is invisible to auditors.

## The approach

1. **Measure actual business health with one metric:** the share of customers who are high-lifetime-value (HLTV), using a rule each company sets, such as spend, equipment purchased or services retained.
2. **Learn from the customers who matter most:** keeping today's high-value customers, and moving those closest to the line into the high-value group.
3. **Adjust based on proven impact:** estimate each agent action's effect on the health score, check it against randomized results, and record every decision as tamper-evident evidence.

## What a working demonstration shows

A simulated year of 50,000 customers, with known true effects, so every result can be checked:

| Result | Simulated outcome |
| --- | --- |
| **The one metric told the truth the dashboard missed** | Traffic and adoption rose all year while the health score fell from 18.7% in May to 15.0% in September after a price increase |
| **Agent contribution measured** | Agents added 0.5–0.8 points to the health score each month, within about 0.1 point of the true values |
| **Spend decisions clarified** | Free shipping added a point of health score for about $400,000; the holiday special cost about $3.8 million per point |
| **Misleading conclusions avoided** | A conventional analysis rated free shipping as harmful; it was one of the most effective actions |
| **Every number traceable** | Any report line drills down to individual verified decision records in five steps |

Results are from simulated data and show the method working, not results for any real business.
